How it began

A reputation earned offline, waiting to be seen online.

Very few businesses reach four decades. The ones that do usually got there by being consistently good at the work and kind to the people in front of them. East St Kilda Dental is one of those.

What changes over forty years is how people choose. A family moving into Elwood or Caulfield today is as likely to type "gentle dentist near me" into a phone, or ask an AI assistant, as they are to ask the neighbours. Someone who has avoided the dentist for five years will read a practice's website very carefully before they are brave enough to call.

The practice's digital presence had simply not kept pace with its reputation. Nothing about that was urgent. It was a question of whether a stranger could see what long-term patients already knew.

The brief: a new digital look that felt as warm and unhurried as a visit, a site built to be found by people who had never heard of the practice, and someone to look after it properly afterwards.

REFERRALS NEVER STOPPED. NEW WAYS OF CHOOSING WERE ADDED ON TOP.
What made it interesting

A dental website is judged by people who are already nervous.

Four things shaped every decision we made.

01

The decision happens before the phone call

Anxious patients read everything first. They want to know what the first visit involves, whether they will be judged, and what it will cost. If the site does not answer those questions, they close the tab and put it off another year.

02

Health advertising has rules

Dental practices sit under the national advertising guidelines for health practitioners. Copy has to be accurate, balanced and free of the promises other industries take for granted. Warmth had to come from tone and clarity rather than claims.

03

Local search works suburb by suburb

People search for a dentist in their own suburb, not in "Melbourne's inner south-east". A practice on a busy corner serves more than twenty surrounding suburbs, and each needed to be discoverable on its own terms.

04

AI assistants now answer first

More and more people ask an AI assistant which dentist to see, or why their gums bleed, before they ever click a link. Being quoted in that answer requires a different kind of preparation from ranking on page one.

How we worked

From listening to looking after it.

Stage 01

Understanding the practice

Time with the team to learn how the practice actually runs: who walks in, what they are worried about, which questions reception answers ten times a day, and what makes this clinic different from the one down the road.

Stage 02

Structure built around patient questions

An information architecture organised the way patients think. By what they need (a first visit, an emergency, a check-up), by concern (bleeding gums, a broken tooth, toothache), by where they live, and by what it will cost.

Stage 03

Content and design

Copy written in the practice's own voice: calm, plain and judgement-free, with the first visit explained in full and fees addressed openly. A soft, warm design that feels closer to a welcoming waiting room than a clinical brochure.

Stage 04

Build and hosting

A fast, modern site built to work well on a phone in a waiting room or a bus shelter, then deployed on hosting KEYOB manages, so performance, security and uptime sit with one accountable team.

Stage 05

SEO, AEO and GEO

Search, answer and generative engine optimisation built in from the start rather than bolted on afterwards. More on this below, because it is where most of the lasting value sits.

Stage 06

Ongoing care

KEYOB continues to maintain the site: updates, content changes, technical health and search performance. A website that is looked after keeps earning; one that is left alone slowly stops.

What we built

A site that answers the question before it is asked.

A first visit explained in full. A dedicated page for the new patient visit sets out what happens, how long it takes and what is included, which removes the single biggest source of hesitation for people who have stayed away.

A home for nervous patients. A page written directly for people who are frightened of the dentist, in language that never makes them feel at fault for how long it has been.

Twenty-two service pages. Preventive, restorative, cosmetic, orthodontic, implant and emergency care, each written to explain rather than sell.

Honest cost information. A fees guide, payment options, the twelve major health funds accepted, and guidance on using superannuation for treatment.

A dental education library. Clear articles on the questions people search at night, from bleeding gums to what to expect after years away.

Booking without friction. Online booking, click-to-call throughout, and a contact form that asks who you are and what you need so the practice can reply properly.

Being found

Three kinds of search, one practice.

A patient can now find a dentist three different ways. We built for all three, and they reinforce each other.

SEO

Ranking in search

"dentist elwood"

Dedicated local pages for twenty surrounding suburbs, each with genuine directions, transport and parking detail rather than a swapped place name. Clean technical foundations, fast pages, local business data and consistent practice details everywhere they appear.

AEO

Being the answer

"why are my gums bleeding?"

Question-led education articles and a dental FAQ, written so a search engine can lift a clear, accurate answer directly. Structured data tells engines exactly what each page is: a service, a question and answer, a local practice.

GEO

Being cited by AI

"gentle dentist for nervous patients near St Kilda?"

Generative assistants recommend what they can understand and verify. Plain facts stated consistently, a clear account of who the practice is for, and substantive content give AI systems something reliable to draw on when they answer.

The turning point

Writing for the patient who has not been in years.

Early on, the conversation shifted from what the practice offers to who is most afraid to walk in. The answer was clear: the person who stopped going years ago and now feels embarrassed about it.

Designing for that person changed everything else. It is why the site talks openly about cost, explains the first visit step by step, and keeps repeating the same quiet message in different ways: however long it has been, you are welcome here. A site that reassures the most hesitant visitor tends to work well for everyone else too.

What this work taught us

A website is not a launch. It is a relationship.

The most valuable part of this engagement is the part nobody sees: the ongoing care. Search engines change how they rank, AI assistants change how they answer, and a practice's services and team evolve. A site that is built and then left starts falling behind the week after launch.

Established businesses do not need to be reinvented. They need their existing strengths made visible, and then someone who stays close enough to keep it that way.
